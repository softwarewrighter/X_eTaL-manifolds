programdemos/representation-explorer/representation-hyperbolic.xtl

Representation explorer, part 2: the Poincare disk, and the tree. The disk's distance (worked in full, and what it does near the rim), a shortest path in it, why a tree wants such a space (the room round a point grows with its distance as the tree's levels do, counted); then the tree of the kit fitted in each of the three spaces at the same dimension, from the same start, by the same steps: the plane (curvature 0), the unit sphere (+1) and the disk (-1), each at the scale the fit chooses; the distortion by step and at the end, the worst pair, a classifier from each embedding, three starts for the spread; and curvature as a control: the same fit at fixed curvatures from +1 through 0 to -4. The second half of H1.

function · line 102

Why a tree wants the disk, counted: level L of a tree of branching b has b^L nodes, every one L edges from the root. Put them evenly round the circle of radius L in the plane (length 2 pi L) and in the disk (2 pi sinh L: its plain radius is tanh(L / 2)); then measure the distance between neighbors along each space (the shortest path between them, which in the disk is far shorter than the arc round the circle). One row per level, 1 to 5: nodes; the two circles' lengths; the spacing of neighbors in the plane and in the disk. In the plane the spacing shrinks with every level and is under an edge's length by level 5; in the disk it grows.

invD : Float

value · line 142

An embedding P of the tree in a space of curvature K is fitted by lowering the mean over pairs of (E / D - 1)^2: D the tree's distances (an edge is 1), E the distances between the embedded nodes along the space. A sphere of curvature K > 0 is the unit sphere with every length times its radius r = 1 / sqrt K; a ball of curvature K < 0 is the unit ball with every length divided by sqrt -K; K = 0 is the plane. So the fit keeps its points in the unit sphere or ball (Manifold's) and scales their distances, and a step at rate lr along the space of curvature K is the unit space's step at rate lr sqrt |K|, with the same W (the loss's gradient with respect to the distances): a length in the space is 1 / sqrt |K| times its unit-space length, which scales the distances up and the coordinates down by the same factor, and only the step's length is left scaled. Each step: W; the gradient along the unit space with respect to every point (Manifold's ...DistancesGradient) for that W; a step against it along the space (P - lr G in the plane, s_phereStep on the sphere, b_allStep in the ball then b_allProject, with how far past the rim the step went kept as the constraint's violation). The scale is not fitted: at a fixed scale the curvature means something (the same tree at the same size in a smaller or larger space); with the scale free, every curvature of one sign is the same space, and a first run with the scale free saw the sphere's fit shrink its embedding without end toward the plane's, which is the sweep's finding below, measured instead of run away with. ᵘm̲easure (D, E): W, and the row (loss, mean distortion, worst pair) at E. (1 / D over the pairs, 0 elsewhere, is the same every step.)

invD ← pairF ÷ T m̲ax 1e-300
