  • library libs/Mixing/src/Mixing.xtl: Mixing: residual streams mixed under a constraint. A residual block carries one stream x and adds what a layer computes from it, x + F(x); hyper-connections (Zhu et al., ICLR 2025) carry n streams side by side, a matrix of n rows, and mix them at every layer by an n by n matrix; mHC (Xie et al., 2025) holds that matrix to the doubly stochastic matrices (no entry negative, every row and every column summing to 1). This library: the layer over n streams as mHC writes it, x <- H x + b F(a' x); the streams' mean and differences; the path from the identity to uniform mixing; and how far a matrix is from each kind of mixing (doubly stochastic, orthogonal), with the gain mHC measures. Sinkhorn's iteration, which makes a positive matrix doubly stochastic, is matrix scaling and general: it is asked of X_eTaL-libraries (docs/xetal-libraries-asks.md, GL3), not kept here. Import it with an alias of your choice: "hc:" u_se< "Mixing". Put libs/Mixing/src on XETAL_PATH ("just path"); the reference is libs/Mixing/docs. Names with l: are exported; those under h: are private to this file.

  • program demos/dynamics-explorer/dynamics-explorer.xtl: Dynamics explorer, part 1: must a state head downhill? A vector field gives a direction and a size at every point of the plane, dx/dt = v(x); a state that follows it traces a path. Five linear fields v(x) = A x (attractor, rotation, spiral, saddle, expanding spiral): the arrows on a grid, the paths from four starts by a fourth-order Runge-Kutta step beside the exact paths, the divergence and curl of each by central differences beside the exact values, the sum of v . dx round a loop (zero for a field that is minus the gradient of a loss, not for one that turns), and the stability of the rest point from the trace and determinant of A. Then integration as a choice: Euler's step against Runge-Kutta's on the rotation, whose exact paths are circles, at four step sizes. And as machine learning meets it: gradient descent on a loss (a gradient field: it settles) beside simultaneous descent and ascent on the two-player objective f(x, y) = x y (a rotation: it spirals out), with two known repairs, the players taking turns and a look-ahead step (extragradient), from the same start at the same rate.
  • program demos/manifold-tour/manifold-tour.xtl: Manifold tour: five spaces this repository's experiments live on, the circle, the sphere, the torus, the Swiss roll and the Poincare disk, each drawn from its map (its own coordinates) into the space around it, and on each one measurement of what a manifold is: two points near each other are nearly as far apart along the space as through it (locally flat), two points far apart are not; on the roll, near through the air can be far along the sheet; on the disk, near the rim a small step on paper is a long way. Nothing is trained.
  • program demos/mhc-explorer/mhc-depth.xtl: mHC explorer, part 2: from matrices to networks. A stack of small residual layers over n streams (mHC's layer, Mixing's s_tack) with four kinds of mixing made from the same trained numbers: the plain residual (the identity), free mixing (hyper-connections), doubly stochastic mixing (Sinkhorn's iteration on exp, mHC's) and orthogonal mixing (the Cayley transform). First at the start of training, depth by depth: the size of the signal going up, of the gradient coming down, the streams' spread, the composite gain and the constraint's violation, over several seeds. Then trained on the kit's flat clusters at four depths: loss by step, held-out accuracy, whether the run stayed finite, the cost of a step. The full training is done offline by this program (train/train.sh) and its records read back from data/; the page trains the first steps at the smallest depth again, and they must equal the records.

  • program demos/mhc-explorer/mhc-explorer.xtl: mHC explorer, part 1: the matrices. When a network carries several streams of numbers side by side and mixes them at every layer, what may the mixing be allowed to do? From one residual stream, x + F(x), to n streams mixed by an n by n matrix (hyper-connections, Zhu et al. 2025; mHC, Xie et al. 2025, its equation 3); the doubly stochastic matrices of size 3, nine entries and four free; the path from the identity to uniform mixing; Sinkhorn's iteration from a free positive matrix, one sweep worked in full; what each kind of matrix does to a signal mixed again and again (doubly stochastic, free, orthogonal); and the set itself, the Birkhoff polytope, with its corners. A single matrix per kind here: actual mHC learns matrices that depend on the input.
  • program demos/optimization-explorer/optimization-explorer.xtl: Optimization explorer, part 1: is downhill one direction? One fixed loss, L(x, y) = (x^2 + 3 y^2) / 2, its contours and its gradient field (a field that comes from a potential); gradient descent on it under different metrics, the same start and the same step size policy: the plain step (the metric is the identity), a step under the metric diag(a, 1) with a as the control, and Newton's direction (the loss's own curvature as the metric). The objective never changes; the path and the number of steps do. The steepest direction at one point under two metrics, worked in full: the unit ball of each and the direction that lowers the loss most within it. Then the same question on a curved space: the unit vector that makes x' A x largest (the leading eigenvector) found by a step that stays on the sphere (Manifold's) against a flat step repaired afterward.
  • program demos/optimization-explorer/optimization-training.xtl: Optimization explorer, part 2: from a bowl to a model. A softmax model small enough that its Fisher matrix can be formed and solved exactly (two inputs, three classes, six weights) is trained on the flat clusters of the kit three ways from the same start on the same split: plain gradient descent, a diagonal preconditioner (the Fisher's diagonal) and the natural-gradient step (the Fisher as the metric, solved with Matrix, damped); once with one input badly scaled on purpose, so the metric has something to fix, and once with the inputs as they are, where it should buy little. Loss by step, held-out accuracy, steps to a target, the size of each update, and the cost of a step counted. Then the Fisher metric as a geometry: one small change of the weights measured by the flat ruler and by the Fisher, and the natural step's indifference to how an input is scaled, run undamped.
  • program demos/representation-explorer/representation-explorer.xtl: Representation explorer, part 1: flat against spherical. The same points kept two ways, as they are (a flat space, the straight line between them) and as their directions on the unit sphere (the angle between them), on two data sets of the kit built with a known structure: flat clusters, whose structure is the straight line's, and directions, whose structure is the angle's. For each: three distances between the same pairs, a shortest path in each space, a mean in each space, nearest neighbors and a decision boundary under each distance, a small classifier trained on each representation (the same network, seeds, split and steps), and how badly each representation keeps the data's own distances. The first test of H1.

The disk's distance (worked in full, and what it does near the rim), a shortest path in it, why a tree wants such a space (the room round a point grows with its distance as the tree's levels do, counted); then the tree of the kit fitted in each of the three spaces at the same dimension, from the same start, by the same steps: the plane (curvature 0), the unit sphere (+1) and the disk (-1), each at the scale the fit chooses; the distortion by step and at the end, the worst pair, a classifier from each embedding, three starts for the spread; and curvature as a control: the same fit at fixed curvatures from +1 through 0 to -4. The second half of H1.

Two ways to measure how far apart two of them are, the straight line through the sphere and the great circle along it, order every point's neighbors the same way; and the plain average of the points is not on the sphere, where the mean along it is.
