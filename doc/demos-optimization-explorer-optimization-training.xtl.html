programdemos/optimization-explorer/optimization-training.xtl

Optimization explorer, part 2: from a bowl to a model. A softmax model small enough that its Fisher matrix can be formed and solved exactly (two inputs, three classes, six weights) is trained on the flat clusters of the kit three ways from the same start on the same split: plain gradient descent, a diagonal preconditioner (the Fisher's diagonal) and the natural-gradient step (the Fisher as the metric, solved with Matrix, damped); once with one input badly scaled on purpose, so the metric has something to fix, and once with the inputs as they are, where it should buy little. Loss by step, held-out accuracy, steps to a target, the size of each update, and the cost of a step counted. Then the Fisher metric as a geometry: one small change of the weights measured by the flat ruler and by the Fisher, and the natural step's indifference to how an input is scaled, run undamped.

ᵘs̲cores : Num a => (a, a) -> a

function · line 78

Softmax regression with the third class's score fixed at 0, so the weights are 2 rows (classes 1 and 2) by 3 (bias, x1, x2), six numbers, and the Fisher matrix is 6 by 6 and not singular (with a row per class the scores could all shift together and the Fisher would have a zero direction). The score of class c is its row dot (1, x1, x2); the probabilities are exp of the scores over their sum with 1 for class 3 (computed from the largest score, so nothing overflows). The loss is the mean cross-entropy over the rows; its gradient with respect to W is (P - Y)' Xb / n, a 2 by 3 matrix.

ᵘb̲lock : (Float, Float) -> Float

function · line 101

The Fisher information of the model at W, the mean over the rows of the expected outer product of the score of the log-likelihood: for softmax regression, the mean over rows of kron(M_i, x_i x_i') with M_i = diag(p_i) - p_i p_i' over the two free classes (Amari 1998; X_eTaL-ML's by GM4, written out here). Written by blocks: the (c, c') block is Xb' diag(M_i[c, c']) Xb / n, three distinct blocks for the 2 by 2 of classes, assembled 6 by 6 in the weights' order (class 1: bias, x1, x2; class 2: the same). For this model the Fisher is also the loss's own curvature (its Hessian), which the checks confirm against central differences of the gradient: so the natural step here is Newton's step on the cross-entropy, a fact of softmax regression and not of networks in general.

ᵘc̲ost : Num a => a -> Float

function · line 154

The cost of a step, counted (analytical): the numbers a step keeps beyond the weights (the gradient's 6; plus the Fisher's diagonal, 6; plus the whole Fisher, 36) and the multiplies: the probabilities and the gradient, 12 per training row for every method (6 for the two scores, 6 for the gradient's outer product); the diagonal adds 12 per row (u:f_isherDiag: the three squared inputs, then each times the two classes' p (1 - p), 3 + 6, and the two p (1 - p), rounded up to 12); the whole Fisher adds 36 per row (u:f_isher: three blocks, (1,1), (1,2) and (2,2), of 3 by 3 = 9 products of an input pair, each times the row's weight, 3 x 9 + 9) and the solve of a 6 by 6 system by elimination, about 6^3 / 3 + 6^2 = 108. Exponentials, divisions and additions are not counted: the exponentials are the same per row for every method. A model with w weights keeps w^2 numbers and solves in w^3 / 3: the numbers of this model hide that, which the page says.
