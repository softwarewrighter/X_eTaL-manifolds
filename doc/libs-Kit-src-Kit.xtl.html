librarylibs/Kit/src/Kit.xtl

Kit: the experiment kit's data sets, each with a known structure and made from its own seed: flat (Gaussian clusters), directional (classes that differ by direction, not length) and hierarchical (a tree, its distances along the tree, a class per branch, and a plain layout of it in the plane); and the seeded numbers they are made of. Import it with an alias of your choice: "kt:" u_se< "Kit". Put libs/Kit/src on XETAL_PATH ("just path"); the reference is libs/Kit/docs. Names with l: are exported; those under h: are private to this file.

A data set is (X, y): X a matrix, one point per row; y its labels, 1 to k. Every set is a function of its seed and its sizes alone (not of the run's seed, nor of what was drawn before it), so two demos asking for the same set get the same numbers: the seed is a whole number, and the generator is Park and Miller's (1988) multiplier 16807 modulo 2^31 - 1, written out here until X_eTaL-libraries' Random takes a seed of its own (docs/xetal-libraries-asks.md, GL8). The comparisons this repository makes are between geometries on the same data: what these functions give is held equal across them.

u̲niform (seed, n): n Floats in [0, 1), each a function of the seed and its place: the Park-Miller sequence x -> 16807 x mod (2^31 - 1) started from 1 + |seed| mod (2^31 - 2) (so seed 0 is a seed like any other and no seed is the sequence's fixed point 0), divided by 2^31 - 1. The same seed gives the same numbers in any run.

Flat clusters

ˡf̲lat : Num a => (Int, Int, Int, Int, a) -> (Float, Int)

function · line 78

f̲lat (seed, k, n, d, spread): k Gaussian clusters of n points each in d dimensions: the k centers are standard normal numbers of the seed scaled by 4 (so clusters sit a few units apart), and each point is its center plus spread times a standard normal vector; (X, y), X of k n rows and y the cluster of each row, 1 to k, in order. The structure is flat: straight-line distance is the right one, and a point's nearest neighbors are mostly its own cluster when spread is well under the centers' spacing.

Directions

ˡd̲irections : Num a => (Int, Int, Int, a) -> (Float, Int)

function · line 107

d̲irections (seed, k, n, cone): k classes of n points in three dimensions that differ by direction and not by length: each class has a direction (a seeded standard normal vector put onto the unit sphere) and each point is that direction plus cone times a standard normal vector, put back onto the sphere, then scaled by a length drawn between 0.5 and 2 (uniform), so the lengths carry nothing about the class; (X, y). The structure is directional: the angle between points is the right distance, and their straight-line distance mixes in the lengths.

      "kt:" u̲se< "Kit"
      (X, y) ← ᵏᵗd̲irections (7, 2, 2, 0.1)
      y
1 1 2 2
      s̲hape X
4 3

A tree

ˡt̲ree : (Int, Int) -> Int

function · line 132

t̲ree (depth, branching): the edges of a rooted tree in which every node but the leaves has branching children and every leaf is depth edges from the root: node 1 is the root and the nodes are numbered level by level, so node i's children are the nodes branching (i - 1) + 2 up to branching i + 1. The edges as Graphs takes them: two rows, from (the parent) over to (the child). Depth 4 and branching 2 give 31 nodes and 30 edges.

ˡt̲reeLayout : (Int, Int) -> Float

function · line 194

t̲reeLayout (depth, branching): a plain picture of the tree in the plane, one row (x, y) per node: the root at the origin, each level a circle of radius its depth, the nodes of a level spread evenly round it in their order, so that each subtree is a wedge (the two children of the root at the top and the bottom, their x cos of a quarter turn, 0 up to the machine's rounding). It is a layout, not an embedding that keeps the tree's distances: the straight-line distance between two nodes of this picture is a poor copy of their distance along the tree (two leaves of neighboring branches are near in the picture and 2 depth apart in the tree), which is what the baseline measures.

                                0.0  0.0

-0.00000000000000018369701987210297 -1.0
